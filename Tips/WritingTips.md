# Writing Tips

Good writing is clear, concise, and effective. Whether you're writing a document, a report, or an email, following a few key principles can significantly improve the quality and impact of your communication.

## Numbers

Consistency is key when writing numbers. While different style guides may have slightly different rules, a widely accepted standard is to spell out small numbers and use numerals for larger ones. This improves readability and maintains a professional tone.

* **Spell out numbers less than 10.** This is the most common and fundamental rule.
  * **Example:** "We have **nine** servers in the test cluster and **three** engineers on call."
  * **Example:** "The project will be completed in **two** phases."

* **Use numerals for numbers 10 and greater.** This keeps your writing from becoming overly verbose.
  * **Example:** "The deployment included **27** new microservices."
  * **Example:** "We received over **300** responses to the survey."

* **Always spell out a number that begins a sentence.** This is a stylistic rule to avoid awkward-looking sentences. If the number is large, consider rephrasing the sentence.
  * **Correct:** "**Fifteen** developers attended the workshop."
  * **Incorrect:** "**15** developers attended the workshop."
  * **Better (for large numbers):** "A total of **1,250** bugs were resolved last quarter." instead of "**One thousand two hundred and fifty** bugs were resolved last quarter."

* **Use numerals for technical and precise measurements.** Even for numbers under ten, use numerals when referring to specific units of measure, percentages, money, or time to ensure clarity.
  * **Example:** "The latency must be under **5** ms."
  * **Example:** "The new algorithm showed a **7%** improvement."
  * **Example:** "The server costs **$9** per hour."
  * **Example:** "The meeting is at **4:00** PM."

## Emails

Emails are a primary form of professional communication, but they are often written in haste. Applying a simple filter to your email habits can save you and your recipients significant time and confusion.

### The KISS Principle: Keep It Simple, Stupid

The core of effective email communication is simplicity. Respect your reader's time by making your message as clear and straightforward as possible.

* **Write a clear and specific subject line.** The subject is the most important part of your email. It should summarize the email's content and, if applicable, state the required action.
  * **Vague:** "Quick Question"
  * **Better:** "Question: Date for Q3 Planning Meeting"
  * **Action-oriented:** "ACTION REQUIRED: Please Approve Vacation Request by EOD Friday"

* **State your main point immediately.** Don't bury your lead under paragraphs of background information. Start with your key message or question in the first sentence or two.
  * **Example:** "I am writing to request a project extension to October 15th."

* **Use short sentences and paragraphs.** Break up large blocks of text. Use bullet points or numbered lists to present complex information in an easy-to-scan format. This makes your email much easier to read and digest, especially on mobile devices.

* **Focus on one topic per email.** If you need to discuss multiple, unrelated topics, send separate emails. This makes the conversation easier to track, file, and respond to.

### Additional Tips for Effective Emails

* **Be polite and professional.** Use greetings and closings (e.g., "Hi Jane," and "Thanks," or "Best regards,"). Your tone should be professional, even in brief messages.
* **Clearly state the desired action.** If you need the recipient to do something, make it obvious. Use phrases like "Could you please..." or "The next step is for you to..."
* **Proofread before you send.** Typos and grammatical errors can make you look unprofessional. Read your email one last time to catch any mistakes. Reading it aloud can help spot awkward phrasing.
* **Think twice before "Reply All."** Only use "Reply All" if every single person on the original email chain truly needs to read your response.
